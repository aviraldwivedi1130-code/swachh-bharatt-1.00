From collection to useful material

Where does collected waste go?

Collected waste is sorted by material and condition. Clean, separated material can become a resource; contaminated or hazardous waste needs safe, approved treatment.

Food and garden waste

Separated organic waste can be composted into soil conditioner or processed in a suitable biogas plant to produce biogas and digestate.

Examples: fruit and vegetable scraps, leaves, grass clippings.

Paper, cardboard, glass and metals

Sorted recyclable materials go to material recovery and recycling facilities, where they can become new paper products, glass items or metal products.

Best step: keep recyclables dry and separate from food waste.

Plastic

Accepted plastic is sorted by type, cleaned and reprocessed into flakes or pellets for new products. Some mixed or soiled plastics may not be recyclable locally.

Examples: bottles, containers and packaging accepted by local facilities.

Construction and demolition debris

Concrete, brick and rubble can be crushed and screened for approved road base, blocks or other construction uses. Reuse depends on quality and local standards.

Keep separate: do not mix rubble with household garbage.

Electronic and battery waste

Electronics and batteries should go to designated collection points or authorized handlers, who recover useful materials and manage hazardous parts safely.

Do not: put batteries or electronics in regular street bins.

Hazardous or biomedical waste

These materials need separate handling and treatment through the appropriate authorized service. They must not be mixed with ordinary recyclables or compost.

Examples: sharps, chemicals, certain medical waste and unknown hazardous items.

Local processing can vary

These are common waste pathways, not a guarantee that every item is recycled. The actual destination depends on local collection, sorting capacity, material condition and authorized facilities. Keep waste separated and follow your Nagar Nigam's collection instructions.
